## Start with scope, not slogans

When people begin researching a fertility specialist, the first things they often notice are the surface details: a clean website, a convenient location, a friendly front desk. Those things matter, but they rarely tell you the most important thing — what the practice actually does, and how that lines up with what you need.

**Service scope** is the practical answer to the question: *What can this provider help with, and where do they hand off to someone else?* Comparing scope is one of the most useful early steps in provider selection because it helps you build a shortlist you can actually evaluate. It also helps you avoid the frustration of a first consultation where you discover the provider does not offer the kind of support you were hoping to explore.

This guide is about how to compare scope. It is not a substitute for medical, financial, or legal advice, and it does not tell you which provider is right for you. Only a qualified professional who knows your situation can do that. Instead, this is a framework for asking clearer questions so you can make a more informed decision.

## What "service scope" actually includes

Scope is more than a list of procedures. It is a combination of several dimensions. When you compare providers, try to look at each dimension separately, because a provider might be strong in one and limited in another.

- **Breadth of services.** What categories of care does the practice offer? Some focus on a narrow set of services; others offer a wider range.
- **Depth within a service.** For any given service, how much of the journey does the provider support — initial evaluation, ongoing monitoring, coordination with other professionals?
- **Care team structure.** Who will you actually interact with? Is there a single primary contact, or does the team rotate? What roles are represented on the team?
- **Continuity and follow-up.** How does the practice stay connected with you between visits? Who answers questions, and through what channel?
- **Coordination with others.** Does the provider work alongside other professionals you may already see, and how is that communication handled?
- **Access and logistics.** Appointment availability, location, remote options, and scheduling patterns all shape whether the scope is usable for your life.
- **Clarity of information.** How clearly does the practice explain what it offers, what it does not offer, and what the next steps look like?

None of these dimensions is inherently better or worse. The goal is to find alignment between what a provider offers and what you are looking for.

## A scope comparison checklist

Use the following checklist as a note-taking tool. You can fill it in for each provider you are considering, then compare side by side. Treat it as a prompt for questions rather than a scorecard with a single right answer.

**1. Service categories**
- What general areas of fertility-related care does this practice focus on?
- Are there services commonly associated with fertility care that this practice does *not* offer?

**2. Depth of support**
- Does the provider describe support across the whole journey, or a specific phase?
- If a phase is outside their scope, who do they typically refer to?

**3. Team and roles**
- Who is the main point of contact?
- What other roles are part of the care team, and how do they interact with you?

**4. Communication and follow-up**
- How are questions handled between appointments?
- What is the expected response pattern, and through which channels?

**5. Coordination**
- How does the practice coordinate with other professionals you may see?
- What information do they typically need from you to coordinate well?

**6. Access and logistics**
- What appointment types are available (in person, remote, or a mix)?
- How far in advance are appointments typically booked?

**7. Information clarity**
- Is the practice's scope described in plain language on its public materials?
- Can you find answers to basic scope questions without a consultation?

**8. Fit with your priorities**
- Which of the above matters most to you right now, and why?
- Which trade-offs are you willing to make, and which are not?

## Hypothetical examples (clearly labeled)

The following scenarios are **hypothetical examples** created to illustrate how scope comparison can work. They are not real providers, not real people, and not recommendations. They do not describe outcomes, costs, or clinical results.

**Example A — The broad-scope practice.**
Imagine a practice that describes itself as offering a wide range of fertility-related services, with a large team and multiple locations. On paper, the breadth is appealing. When you look closer, you notice that appointments may be spread across different team members, and continuity depends on scheduling. Your checklist question becomes: *How do I keep a consistent thread across visits?* If continuity is a top priority for you, this is a trade-off to weigh.

**Example B — The focused practice.**
Imagine a smaller practice that describes a narrower scope, with a single primary contact and a consistent team. The breadth is smaller, but the continuity may be clearer. Your checklist question becomes: *If my needs extend beyond this scope, how does the practice handle that?* If you value a close working relationship and are comfortable with a narrower scope, this might align well.

**Example C — The mixed-access practice.**
Imagine a practice that offers a blend of in-person and remote appointments, with a detailed public description of how each is used. Your checklist question becomes: *Which parts of my care can happen remotely, and which require in-person visits?* If travel or scheduling is a major factor for you, this dimension may carry extra weight.

In each example, the point is not that one practice is better. The point is that the *same* practice can look different depending on which scope dimensions matter most to you.

## Turning scope into better questions

Once you have a rough comparison, convert your notes into questions you can ask directly. Good questions are specific, open-ended, and focused on process rather than promises.

- "Can you walk me through what your practice typically helps with, and what falls outside your scope?"
- "Who would be my main point of contact, and how consistent is that over time?"
- "How do you coordinate with other professionals I might already be seeing?"
- "What does follow-up look like between appointments?"
- "How would you describe the trade-offs of your approach compared with a broader or narrower scope?"

Notice that none of these questions ask for a guarantee or a prediction. They ask for a description of how the practice works. That is the kind of information you can compare across providers.

## Common pitfalls when comparing scope

- **Confusing breadth with fit.** A longer list of services does not automatically mean a better match. Fit depends on your priorities.
- **Overlooking logistics.** Even an excellent scope is hard to use if scheduling or location does not work for you.
- **Assuming continuity.** Ask explicitly how the team is structured, rather than assuming you will see the same person each time.
- **Skipping the hand-off question.** Knowing who a provider refers to when something is outside their scope tells you a lot about how they work.
- **Treating marketing language as scope.** Phrases like "comprehensive" or "personalized" are common. Ask what they mean in practice.

## Where this fits in your decision

Scope comparison is an early filter, not a final decision. It helps you narrow a long list into a shortlist worth a conversation. From there, you can explore other dimensions — how you communicate with the team, how information is shared, and what ongoing support looks like.
